The community is where the tools meet people. ISRD's tools are designed to interoperate: they share a common way of holding what you make, so that a model, a plan or a piece of research made by one person can be picked up, tested and built on by another. The community is the set of people and projects who do that together: applied systems thinkers, tool builders, project leaders, and people just getting started.

What it is for

Systemic change takes many hands, in many roles, in many fields. The community is meant to let people work together with the tools: share what they make, borrow from a shared library, comment on each other's projects, say how well the tools work and what would make them better, and, in time, help choose which new features get built.

How it will work

  • A shared library of models, maps, templates and practice cases that members can borrow and add to.
  • Linked projects. Each project has its own community around its issue, and projects can link to one another, so that work on one issue can inform another.
  • Views for each role. People bring different parts to a change, and see the same shared work in the way their part needs.
  • A directory of members who choose to be listed.

The idea follows a design the founder studied in 1995 for connecting the many tools of an education system, and extends it from education to systemic change in any field.

What membership is not

It is not a place for experts to talk over the heads of everyone else. It will run mostly in writing, at your own pace. Members will help look after it.

And later

A conference, once there is something worth meeting about.

If you would like to hear when membership opens, write to us.
