Tools

Tools for systemic change

A suite for people who help others change a system.

A suite of tools for people who help others change a system. A model shows what a choice might do. A studio helps you think it through and put it in words. Each does one job well and hands its result to the next. We build them so that you can see how they work, and we say plainly which ones are ready and which are planned.

Who the suite is for

The suite is built for the person who helps a group change a system: a change agent, consultant, analyst, project leader, planner, researcher, entrepreneur, inventor or opinion leader, working with the people who live inside the problem. The Start here guide is the front door for everyone else.

How the tools connect

The tools are designed to work as one system and not as separate products.

  • A common format. Models, maps, plans, sources and drafts are kept in a shared form. A result made in one tool can be opened in another, and passed to another person. (Planned)
  • A shared library. A public shelf of parts that people can borrow and add to: models, maps, templates and practice cases. It saves starting from nothing, shows the real differences between approaches, and helps a field settle on common terms. (Planned)
  • A view for each role. The same information, shown to each person as their part of the work needs it: the analyst, the project lead, the neighbour, the funder. The views change as the work changes. (Planned)
  • A community. People and projects linked through the tools, so that many people can pull in the same direction. See Community. (Planned)

This follows the founder's 1995 design study of a connected system of tools for education. The present work extends the idea beyond education, to systemic change in any field.

The work, and the tool for each part

The suite is arranged in the order the work usually goes. A designer moves back and forth between these; the order is not a march.

The work What you need The tool Status
1. Understand the situation Gather what is known, who is affected, and what is claimed; keep sources and notes together Writing Studio: notes, sources, chats, review Live
2. Draw the system Show the parts, the connections, the loops and the delays Writing Studio: maps Live
3. Find where it could change Test ideas about leverage against a model Models you can run; the model builder Models live; builder planned
4. Explore options with the people involved Let others try the model and say what they see Shared models and project pages Planned
5. Decide and plan Turn the choice into a plan, with the reasoning and the evidence label Writing Studio: drafts, versions Live
6. Explain it to others Briefs, stories, proposals, a primer for the issue Writing Studio Live
7. Build the skills of the people who carry it on Practise the method on a small invented case Practice games Planned
8. Share and keep it going A library of the works and models, links to other projects Library; projects Planned
9. Find out whether it worked Record what changed and learn from it A way to record expectations and results Not yet designed

The tools

Each tool is described the same way: when you would use it, what you do, what you leave with, and whether it is live or planned.

Writing Studio (live)

Use it when you have a big, complicated piece of work and too many pieces to hold: a report, a proposal, a story, a plan. You do start with a note, collect sources, map how the parts connect, draft in your own words, and test the draft with the reviewer. You leave with a finished piece, the sources behind it, and a picture of how it fits together.

It is a place to learn to write well, and to do big, complicated pieces of writing. It shows how the parts of a piece fit together, helps you practise, keeps your notes, sources and drafts in one place, and keeps the writing yours. When the AI offers words, they are only a sketch for you to test and rewrite. Open the Writing Studio →

A sample tool. The Writing Studio is the one tool in the suite you can use for real work today, so it also shows how every tool is meant to behave: one expert process in small steps, help at each step, the whole project in one place, and you in charge. See how it works as a loop.

Models you can run (live)

Use it when you want to feel how a structure behaves: how a loop settles, how a rumour spreads, how a small difference grows. You do change a setting and watch what the system does. You leave with a sense of why it behaves that way, and questions to take to your own situation. Run a model →

Make a model of your own situation (planned)

A guided way to build a simple model of your own community, team or problem, without writing code. It is not built yet. We will say here when it is.

One studio for many kinds of expert work (planned)

The Writing Studio is the first of what we call a core studio: a general tool that takes an expert process, breaks it into small steps, helps with each, and adds a working canvas and models for applying systems thinking to the work. A journalist, a city planner, a fiction writer, an engineer and a biologist do different work. The tool is the same; the setup is theirs, chosen from a short set of questions at the start.

It is not a tool that does everything, and not a tool that makes software on demand. It is one tool, set up for your work.

Templates (planned)

A few ready-made setups for commonly requested uses, such as research synthesis or writing a proposal, and, as we learn what people need, for particular topics. Any template can be changed to fit your own field.

Practice games and the library (planned)

Practice games let you try an expert skill on a small invented case before the real one. The library lets you gather and share your finished works and models, with your readers and with other projects.

Honest edges

  • Step 9 is a gap. Nobody can know a change worked without recording what was expected and what happened. We want to build it, and until it exists we do not claim it does.
  • Steps 4 and 8 depend on sharing between people, and so does the community. Today the tools are used one person at a time, and the common format, the library and the role views are designs, not features.
  • The hand-offs between tools ("take this result into a note", "test this idea in a model") are not built. Models and the Studio are separate places today.
  • Pricing is not settled yet. We will say plainly what is free and what is paid before we ask anyone to pay.

What the suite is not

It is not an application that does the systems work for you. Your judgment, the community's knowledge and the field's experts decide what is true and what to do. The tools organize, simulate and train.
