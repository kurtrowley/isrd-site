# Piles and flows

Think about your notes. Every day some come in: an idea in the shower, a line from a book, half a sentence on your phone. Every day some go out: you use one, file one, throw one away.

The pile you have right now is a **stock**. What comes in and what goes out are **flows**.

If more comes in than goes out, the pile grows. Nobody has to be doing anything wrong. That is just how piles work. Many people have a pile that only ever grows, and they feel worse about it each year.

Here is the odd part. The pile is not the problem. The flows are. So the fix is not to try harder to ignore the pile. It is to change how things leave it.

## Try it, on paper

Draw a box and label it "my notes". Draw an arrow into the box for what comes in, and an arrow out for what goes out. Write down roughly how many notes travel along each arrow in a week. Is the pile growing, shrinking or steady? Now ask what would change the pile most: a faster way in, or a slower way out?

## Where else?

A waiting list at a clinic. A river behind a dam. The trust a team has in its manager. Each is a stock with flows. Name one in your own life.
