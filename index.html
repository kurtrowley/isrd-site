Interdisciplinary Systems R&D

Turn systems thinking into systemic change.

Many of us can see that the problems we care about are tangled together: health with housing, housing with jobs, jobs with the climate. Seeing that is the start. Changing it is harder, because real change takes more than one skill. It takes research, planning, modelling, persuading, writing, building, and keeping at it, and each of those is a craft with its own methods. It also takes a safe, systematic way of changing things: small steps, watching what happens, and adjusting, so that we keep our course and cause fewer side effects.

That way of working is not new. Engineers, doctors and farmers use it every day, and we did not invent it. What is new is applying it to the largest changes the Earth now needs.

This site is about tools for systemic change. They support many of those crafts, each one in the way an expert actually does it, and each one looking at the whole situation and not only a piece of it. Until now, tools could not hold that many connected things at once. Now, with AI, we think a connected set of tools can. It is a new kind of tool stack, and we are building it in the open.

We are also building a community around it, so that people can work together, not alone. The tools are designed to interoperate: a model, a plan or a piece of research made by one person can be picked up, tested and built on by another, and many people can pull in the same direction. That is how change gets large enough to matter. The community is still planned; the tools it will connect are being built first.

It is for people who want to change things: change agents, opinion leaders, innovators, entrepreneurs, inventors, and anyone who has seen a problem and wants to do something about it. You do not need a background in systems science. The guide here starts from everyday examples.

Because the tools are new, we also ask a hard question and answer it in public: do these tools help people change things for the better? We build them, we use them, and we test them.

Some tools are ready to use now (models you can run and the Writing Studio). Others are planned. Every page says which.

Applied research

We test the tools in public

ISRD does applied systems research. We build tools by experimental design: we make a version, put it in people’s hands, collect their feedback as they use it, and adjust it, again and again, until it works. We take what the systems sciences already know, apply it to real problems, and say in public what we find.

Mission

Everyone gets a voice

AI can give everybody a voice: a say in how Earth's resources are managed and where our civilization goes next.

ISRD aims to help many producers, especially people who need AI support to be heard, and to keep the picture pluralistic: many perspectives, understood together.
